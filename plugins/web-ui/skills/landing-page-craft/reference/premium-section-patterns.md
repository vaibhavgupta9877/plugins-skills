# Premium section patterns (2026)

Structural patterns that recur on award-grade marketing sites. Each is a **shape for content that already exists** — never invent content to fill one. Visual values (faces, colours, easings) come from the project's `Design language` section, not from here.

## Hero shapes (pick one from the premise)

| Shape | What it is | Use when | Example grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type-as-image** | One claim set at 6–10rem, tight tracking, maybe a single italic word; almost nothing else | The claim is strong and short; no product visual yet | Boon — "Cause the Effect" |
| **Artifact hero** | The real object (book, device, packaging, UI) staged and lit, claim beside it | A physical or visual product exists | Money in Check — the knight as LCP image |
| **World hero** | A persistent scene (canvas/video) the story scrolls over | A place/material is native to the subject; perf budget allows | Oxigen — voxel terrain |
| **Roster / system hero** | The offer shown as its units (team, agents, modules) with a character | Services or multi-part products | Cloudstudio — staff passes + mascot |
| **Maxim hero** | A craft sentence + name, work immediately below | Portfolios and studios | Marcus Lorenzet — "Pixels are small / details are not" |

Every shape still answers *what is this, for whom, what next* within 5 seconds on a phone, with one primary CTA.

## Mid-page patterns

- **Numbered buckets** — `01 / 02 / 03` offers, each with scope, timeline, and price or "from" price when real. Replaces three feature cards.
- **Index rows** — full-width list: index, title, meta (year/sector/role), arrow; hover reveals media on pointer devices. Replaces card grids for work, articles, case studies.
- **Method in N moves** — 3–4 named steps with a verb each ("Diagnose → Decide → Build → Hand over"). Replaces "How it works".
- **Statement interlude** — one sentence at display size between dense sections to reset rhythm. Use at most twice.
- **Roster / people** — real names, roles, photos; staff-pass or contact-sheet treatment if it fits the premise.
- **Proof in place** — a testimonial or metric sits directly under the claim it proves, with attribution.
- **Spec sheet** — mono table of real facts (formats, integrations, SLAs, certifications like ISO/IEC 27001 — only if held).
- **FAQ as conversation** — short, direct answers; the tone of the rest of the page.

## Closing patterns

- **Final CTA mirrors the hero promise** in fewer words.
- **Footer as finale** — oversized wordmark or closing line, one contact action (email/WhatsApp/booking), real socials, legal links. "Back to top" as a designed element on long pages.

## Navigation

- Logo left, one outlined or capsule CTA right, 2–4 links in mono uppercase. Full-screen menu overlay only when links exceed ~5.
- Header inverts ink when crossing sheets of opposite brightness (check contrast in both states).
- Index labels (`[01] Work`) can double as in-page anchors.

## Anti-patterns specific to landing pages

Three equal feature cards; "Trusted by" marquee of unverified logos; a stats band of round numbers; testimonial carousel at the bottom; two equal CTAs in the hero; a section titled "Features". See `product-direction/skills/art-direction/reference/anti-slop.md` for the full gate.
