# Reference case-study principles

**Research date:** 2026-09-27. These are source-attributed observations and transfer lessons, not visual recipes. References are not assets or instructions to copy.

## Money in Check and Cloudstudio

Cloudstudio's case study describes the Money in Check book site as a chess game: board coordinates, algebraic notation, banknote-sculpted pieces, editorial typography, marginalia, and scroll-revealed narrative. Real book spreads, formats, and purchase actions are integrated into that premise. The stated brief rejected a generic bookshop page and finance-site treatment.

**Transferable:** start from the product's actual subject or user truth; when a metaphor is genuinely native to it, let that idea connect content, visual language, interaction, and conversion rather than adding a decorative theme. Show real artifacts and the actual offer. Make commerce part of the story only where it helps the user.

**Do not transfer:** chess imagery, banknote sculpture, its copy, type choices, cursor, or scroll sequence to another product. A daily financial dashboard is not a novel launch page.

Cloudstudio's agency site supports its promise with named services, process steps, delivery details, and selected work. This makes a services proposition more concrete than a generic list of benefits. The extracted text showed several animated counters as zero; validate live/static states before treating dynamic counters as evidence.

## The Power of Storytelling — Awwwards Site of the Day

The Awwwards entry describes a studio story expressed through motion, interaction, and immersive design. It lists separate design/usability/creativity/content scoring and a technical accessibility score of 6.4/10. Those ratings are a reminder that award recognition is not an accessibility or usability guarantee.

**Transferable:** motion can carry a narrative when the surface is meant to be explored. Evaluate accessibility, performance, and task fit separately; do not infer production quality from the award badge alone.

## More Nutrition

The Awwwards case study identifies this as a self-initiated product concept. It describes controlled product staging and a scroll-linked frame sequence; the team changed from swapping image sources to drawing frames on canvas after fast scrolling became laggy.

**Transferable:** authored product imagery and motion can make a real product feel tangible, but the motion system must be measured on real devices and optimized for input speed. Preserve the project's provenance; concept work is not customer evidence.

## Wembi

The studio case describes a modular sign expanded into a visual identity system, with 3D used to make abstract technology easier to understand.

**Transferable:** a distinctive visual system can grow from a product-relevant mark, and 3D earns its cost when it explains something otherwise hard to see. Neither a modular logo nor 3D is a default requirement.

## Decision checks

- Is the idea grounded in this product's actual task, audience, materials, or evidence?
- Does it clarify the primary task, or only make the screen more theatrical?
- Are the cited metrics, customer stories, and product artifacts verified and permissioned?
- Does it remain usable without motion, under reduced motion, on small screens, and on repeat visits?
- Is a specific reference being studied through Tastemaker, or are only user-provided preferences available? State that distinction.
