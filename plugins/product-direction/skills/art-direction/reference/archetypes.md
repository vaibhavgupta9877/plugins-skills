# Design-language archetypes

Starting grammars, not skins. Each names **when it fits**, the grammar it implies, and how it fails. Pick the one whose *reason* matches the subject, then replace every specific (faces, colours, metaphor) with ones native to this product. Combining two is fine; combining four is a collage. If none fits, derive a new one from the premise — these are examples, not a menu.

---

## A. Editorial Artifact — *the object is the interface*
**Seen in:** Money in Check (a finance novel as a chess game).
**Fits:** books, publications, cultural launches, physical products with a strong native system (notation, chapters, formats, recipes, maps).
- **Premise:** the product's own internal system becomes navigation and labels (board coordinates, algebraic notation, page numbers, catalogue codes).
- **Type:** high-contrast editorial serif with ultralight + italic cuts; optional expressive accent face used once (brush script on the curtain).
- **Colour:** paper ground (`#e9e9e9`-ish grey or bone), black ink, one accent drawn from the subject (banknote green).
- **Layout:** marginalia, spreads, captions, running heads; real artifact imagery as LCP.
- **Motion:** editorial tempo; a meaningful curtain; scroll-revealed narrative; overshoot only on small pieces.
- **Fails when:** the metaphor is decorative rather than native, or commerce is buried under story.

## B. Playful Operator — *serious delivery, human personality*
**Seen in:** Cloudstudio (AI studio with a mascot, staff-pass roster, physics pills, six pastel themes).
**Fits:** studios, dev tools, AI products, B2B services that want warmth without losing credibility.
- **Premise:** the offer as a team/roster/system with a character (agents as staff with ID badges).
- **Type:** friendly-quirky grotesk display (Bricolage-like) + monospace as the dominant voice for labels, metrics, and nav.
- **Colour:** one flat saturated pastel ground + near-black ink + paper; optional theme switcher as a toy.
- **Layout:** numbered buckets (01/02/03) with fixed timelines and prices; live-looking metrics in mono (`$0.0142 / run`) — only if real.
- **Motion:** snappy tempo; expo-out for UI, back-out overshoot for toys; one physics or drag toy.
- **Fails when:** the personality outruns the proof, or the metrics are decorative fakes.

## C. Cinematic Monolith — *gravity through scale and restraint*
**Seen in:** Boon (behavioural-intelligence defence tech: "Cause the Effect").
**Fits:** defence, security, deep tech, infrastructure, high-stakes enterprise.
- **Premise:** one enormous claim; the typography is the image.
- **Type:** bespoke or heavy display at ~10rem with negative tracking clamped to size; Swiss grotesk body.
- **Colour:** bone/cream ground, near-black ink, one signal accent (orange/red) used like a warning light.
- **Layout:** full-bleed statement sections alternating with tight capability lists; strong verbs as headings.
- **Motion:** slow-in hard-settle easing, restrained reveals, a WebGL or Lottie element that visualises the abstract capability.
- **Fails when:** scale substitutes for content, or the signal colour is overused until it stops signalling.

## D. Crafted Portfolio — *the site is the sample*
**Seen in:** Marcus Lorenzet (web & product designer: "Pixels are small / details are not").
**Fits:** designers, developers, studios, agencies, personal brands.
- **Premise:** a craft maxim; the work does the imagery.
- **Type:** clean geometric/humanist sans at large sizes; bracketed uppercase labels (`[CASE STUDIES]`) as the system.
- **Colour:** warm near-black (`#11100d`) and sand/beige accents — warm neutrals, never blue-grey.
- **Layout:** featured case studies as large numbered entries, testimonials next to work, "Next project" chaining, back-to-top.
- **Motion:** shared-element transition from project thumbnail to case-study hero; hover-revealed media on lists.
- **Fails when:** transitions are slower than the visitor's curiosity, or case studies lack outcomes.

## E. Atmospheric Terrain — *a world behind the words*
**Seen in:** Oxigen (Gulf technology advisory: voxel terrain in Three.js behind scrolling chapters).
**Fits:** advisory, consulting, regional/place-rooted brands, research institutions, climate/energy.
- **Premise:** a place or landscape native to the subject, rendered as a persistent scene.
- **Type:** refined old-style serif headlines (Iowan-like) at 400 + clean sans + mono uppercase eyebrows at +0.28em.
- **Colour:** dark-first deep ground (teal `#112228`), warm off-white ink, gold and teal accents, hairline rules.
- **Layout:** chapters alternating left/right/center over a fixed canvas; numbered 4-step method; header flips over light sheets.
- **Motion:** cinematic tempo, soft ease-out 1s cards, camera path mapped to scroll; mobile scrims for legibility.
- **Fails when:** the scene costs more than the message, or has no poster fallback for low-end devices.

## Extensions (not from the five references)

## F. Swiss Index — *information as architecture*
**Fits:** data, finance, research, archives, B2B platforms with real depth.
Grotesk + mono, strict 24-col grid, tables and index rows as primary layout, one accent, near-zero decoration, motion only for state. Fails when it becomes a spreadsheet with no hierarchy.

## G. Raw Brutalist — *exposed structure as honesty*
**Fits:** indie/dev tools, experimental studios, counter-positioning against polished competitors.
Monospace-led, visible borders, system colours used deliberately, instant interactions, no smooth scroll. Fails when rawness hides poor hierarchy or accessibility.

## H. Luxury Quiet — *space as the price signal*
**Fits:** fashion, hospitality, architecture, premium goods.
Large imagery, tiny type, extreme whitespace, slow cross-fades, one serif, almost no UI chrome. Fails when navigation disappears or images are slow.

---

## Choosing

1. What does the visitor need to *believe*? (competence → B/D/F; gravity → C/E; desire → A/H; honesty → G)
2. What native system or material does the subject own? That decides the premise, not the archetype.
3. How often will the visitor return? Frequent use pushes toward F/G tempo regardless of marketing flair.
